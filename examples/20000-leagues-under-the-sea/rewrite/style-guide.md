# Style Guide: Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea

## Overview

This is an adaptation of Jules Verne's classic 1870 adventure novel, originally translated from French into Victorian-era English. The rewrite modernizes the prose while preserving the wonder, scientific curiosity, and adventure that define the book.

**Target audience**: General readers, young adult and up -- matching the original's audience. This is NOT a children's edition and NOT an adult-only edition. The original has no explicit violence, sexuality, or mature language, and the rewrite must not introduce any.

---

## Voice & Tone

### Narrator (Professor Aronnax)
- **Original**: Formal Victorian academic prose. Long compound sentences, passive constructions, archaic phrasing ("it was signalised by," "I could scarcely believe it possible").
- **Rewrite**: Modern first-person. Still educated and observant, but conversational rather than stuffy. Aronnax is a scientist who genuinely delights in what he sees -- let that excitement come through naturally rather than through exclamation marks and breathless interjections.
- **Key shift**: Replace Victorian formality with clarity. Aronnax should sound like a well-read modern narrator recounting an extraordinary experience -- think Bill Bryson describing the ocean floor, or a smart documentary voiceover.
- Avoid: Dumbing down. Aronnax is a professor; he can use precise vocabulary. Just don't bury it in 19th-century sentence architecture.

### Dialogue
- **Captain Nemo**: Commanding, philosophical, occasionally poetic. His speeches should feel weighty without being stilted. He speaks with conviction and authority. Modernize syntax but preserve his gravity and emotional restraint -- he reveals himself through rare cracks in his composure, not through long monologues. When he does speak at length, it should feel earned.
- **Ned Land**: Blunt, impatient, physical. He is a man of action stuck in a world of observation. His dialogue should feel natural and direct -- contractions, short sentences, occasional dry humor. He grounds the narrative in practical reality.
- **Conseil**: Formal and loyal, with a dry wit that comes from his habit of classifying everything. His speech should retain a slight formality (he addresses Aronnax as "sir" or "Professor") but feel endearing rather than robotic. His taxonomic digressions can be played for gentle comedy.
- **General dialogue rule**: Cut "said I" / "replied he" inversions. Use "I said" / "he replied" or action beats. Trim redundant dialogue tags where the speaker is clear from context.

---

## Pacing & Structure

- **Long scientific catalogs**: The original frequently lists dozens of species, geological formations, or navigational coordinates. These are a signature of Verne and should NOT be eliminated -- but they should be trimmed and made vivid. Instead of listing 30 species by Latin name, select the most evocative 5-8 and describe them with sensory detail. The reader should feel wonder, not boredom.
- **Navigational passages**: Coordinates, distances, and course changes are frequent. Keep the key ones that orient the reader geographically, but cut or condense passages that are purely mechanical position updates.
- **Chapter rhythm**: Each chapter should have a clear dramatic arc or revelation. Some original chapters meander; tighten them around their central event or insight.
- **Paragraph length**: Break up the original's long paragraphs. Modern readers benefit from shorter paragraphs, especially during dialogue and action sequences.

---

## What to Preserve

1. **The sense of wonder**: This book is about seeing impossible things for the first time. Every underwater scene should make the reader feel what Aronnax feels -- awe.
2. **Nemo's mystery**: His identity, motivations, and past should remain ambiguous for most of the book, revealed in fragments. The rewrite should preserve this drip-feed of information.
3. **The tension of captivity**: The trio's situation -- comfortable prisoners with no hope of release -- is the emotional engine. Keep the claustrophobia, the arguments about escape, the shifting loyalties.
4. **Scientific authenticity**: Verne grounded his fiction in real science. When the science is correct, preserve it. When it is outdated, a footnote is better than rewriting the plot.
5. **The relationship dynamics**: Aronnax torn between wonder and freedom; Ned Land's restless anger; Conseil's calm loyalty; Nemo's isolating grandeur.
6. **Geographic scope**: The journey spans the globe. Maintain the sense of a world-spanning voyage.

---

## What to Change Freely

1. **Victorian sentence structure**: Untangle subordinate clauses. Split long sentences. Remove unnecessary qualifiers ("I must confess that," "it seemed to me that").
2. **Passive voice**: Convert to active wherever it improves clarity and energy.
3. **Archaic vocabulary**: Replace "signalised," "lucubrations," "procès verbal," "cetaceous creature" with modern equivalents -- unless the word adds genuine flavor (keep "narwhal," "cachalot" with context).
4. **Translation artifacts**: The original English text is a Victorian-era translation from French, and it shows. Smooth out awkward constructions that feel like literal translations.
5. **Repetitive descriptions**: Aronnax frequently restates things. Cut redundancy.
6. **Measurement units**: The original mixes metric and imperial unpredictably. Standardize to metric with occasional imperial for accessibility, or let Aronnax naturally use both as a European scientist might.
7. **Outdated cultural references**: Handle with footnotes rather than rewriting. If a reference is obscure but adds nothing, it can be cut.

---

## Formatting Conventions

- Ship names in italics: *Nautilus*, *Abraham Lincoln*, *Scotia*
- Foreign phrases in italics with translation on first use: *savants* (scholars)
- Preserve chapter numbering system (Part One / Part Two with Roman numerals)
- Dates and coordinates should be kept where they anchor the narrative timeline

---

## Content Boundaries

- **No added violence**: The original has a few dramatic scenes (the squid attack, the ramming of the warship) but is not graphic. Match this level.
- **No added romance or sexuality**: None exists in the original; do not introduce it.
- **No modern profanity**: Ned Land can be rough-spoken but uses period-appropriate exclamations ("Confound it!" can become "Damn it" at most).
- **No political editorializing**: Nemo's anti-colonial and anti-oppressor sentiments are part of his character. Present them as his views, not as authorial commentary.

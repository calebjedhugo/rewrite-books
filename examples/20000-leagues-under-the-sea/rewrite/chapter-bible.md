# Chapter Bible: Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea

This is the single source of truth for consistency across all chapter rewrites.

---

## Characters

### Professor Pierre Aronnax (Narrator)
- **Role**: First-person narrator, protagonist
- **Description**: French professor of natural history at the Museum of Paris. Educated, observant, prone to wonder. Physically unremarkable -- his mind is his defining trait.
- **Age**: Not specified; implied middle-aged academic
- **Voice**: Scholarly but passionate. Torn between scientific curiosity (which makes captivity bearable) and moral obligation to seek freedom. Genuinely fascinated by what Nemo shows him, which complicates his desire to escape.
- **Key trait**: He is seduced by knowledge. His weak point -- which he admits -- is that the marvels of the sea almost compensate for his loss of liberty.
- **Relationships**: Master to Conseil (devoted), friend to Ned Land (strained by differing priorities), complex admiration/fear toward Nemo.

### Captain Nemo
- **Role**: Commander of the Nautilus, antagonist/anti-hero
- **Full name**: Never revealed. "Nemo" is Latin for "no one."
- **Description**: Tall, large forehead, straight nose, clearly cut mouth, fine taper hands. Eyes set far apart with extraordinary range of vision. Could be 35-50 years old. Striking, intense presence.
- **Nationality**: Deliberately ambiguous. Speaks French, English, German, Latin fluently. Aronnax suspects "southern" origins. The book never resolves this (Verne later revealed him as Indian in *The Mysterious Island*).
- **Voice**: Calm and commanding, with occasional volcanic emotion. Speaks in carefully constructed sentences. Rarely raises his voice, but when he does, the effect is devastating. Has a habit of falling into brooding silences.
- **Philosophy**: Has "broken all ties of humanity." Considers himself free from human laws. Loves the sea as his true country. Harbors deep hatred -- either "monstrous or sublime" -- against some unnamed oppressor or nation.
- **Key moments of revelation**: His speech about the sea as freedom (ch10), his emotion at the coral cemetery (ch23), his aid to a pearl diver (ch26), his grief over a dying crewman (ch23), the mysterious nighttime incident requiring drugging the passengers (ch22-23), his sinking of the warship (ch44), his final words/playing the organ in anguish (ch45).
- **Motto/Emblem**: "Mobilis in Mobili" (Moving within the moving element) and the letter "N"

### Conseil
- **Role**: Aronnax's servant and loyal companion
- **Full name**: Conseil (no surname given; it means "counsel" in French)
- **Description**: Flemish, 30 years old. Phlegmatic, methodical, devoted. Would follow Aronnax anywhere without question.
- **Voice**: Formal and classified. Habitually refers to Aronnax as "sir" or "master." Has a mania for taxonomic classification -- will categorize creatures mid-crisis. This is played for gentle humor. Speaks in the third person occasionally ("If master permits").
- **Key trait**: Unflappable loyalty. He volunteers for every danger because Aronnax is going. His calm counterbalances Ned's volatility.
- **Languages**: French, German, and some Latin

### Ned Land
- **Role**: Master harpooner, voice of escape
- **Full name**: Ned Land
- **Description**: Canadian, about 40 years old, over six feet tall, strongly built. Grave, taciturn, occasionally violent, very passionate when contradicted. Bold look.
- **Family origin**: From Quebec, originally French-Canadian fishing family.
- **Voice**: Blunt, practical, impatient. Speaks with directness and occasional dry wit. Uses exclamations when frustrated. Not intellectual but not stupid -- his expertise is physical and practical.
- **Key trait**: He never accepts captivity. Where Aronnax is seduced by wonder and Conseil follows his master, Ned represents the primal need for freedom. He grows increasingly desperate, angry, and volatile as months pass.
- **Relationship to food**: Running joke -- Ned constantly craves "real" food (bread, meat, wine) and despises the all-seafood diet. Going ashore means hunting opportunities.
- **Relationship to Nemo**: Hostility from the start. Nemo addresses him as "Master Land" with cold formality.

### Captain Farragut
- **Role**: Commander of the USS *Abraham Lincoln*
- **Appears in**: ch03-ch07 (Part One)
- **Description**: A good seaman, absolutely convinced the monster exists. Single-minded determination to destroy it.
- **Fate**: Left behind when Aronnax, Ned, and Conseil fall overboard during the battle with the "monster."

### The Second-in-Command (of the Nautilus)
- **Description**: Short, very muscular, broad-shouldered, thick moustache, quick penetrating look, "Southern French" vivacity
- **Role**: Carries out Nemo's orders, makes daily observations on the platform, speaks the mysterious language "Nautron respoc lorni virch."
- **Name**: Never given

### The Crew of the Nautilus
- **Number**: Estimated at about 10 crew members (possibly more)
- **Description**: Silent, obedient, speak an unknown language. Their nationality is never identified. Completely loyal to Nemo.
- **Notable moments**: One crewman is fatally wounded in the mysterious nighttime incident (ch22-23) and buried in the coral cemetery.

## The Nautilus

### Physical Description
- Cigar/cylinder-shaped submarine vessel
- 70 meters long, 8 meters at widest beam
- Displacement: 1,500 tons
- Maximum depth: undetermined but very deep
- Speed: up to 50 knots (extraordinary)
- Hull: double-layered with riveted iron plates
- Armed with a steel spur/ram at the prow

### Interior Spaces (as encountered)
1. **The prison/first cabin**: Where the trio is initially held. Iron walls, wooden table, stools, phormium mats.
2. **The saloon/drawing-room**: 30x18x15 feet. Luminous ceiling, museum of natural specimens, paintings by old masters, pipe organ, statues, pearl collection.
3. **The library**: 12,000 volumes, divans, reading desks, electric light.
4. **The dining room**: Oaken sideboards with china/porcelain, center table.
5. **Aronnax's cabin**: Elegant room with bed and dressing table.
6. **Nemo's cabin**: Severe, monkish. Small iron bedstead, table, toilet articles. Sparse.
7. **The platform**: Open deck on top for observation.
8. **The machinery room**: Where the electrical systems are housed.
9. **The pilot house**: Glass cage where the steersman operates.

### Power & Technology
- Powered entirely by electricity generated from seawater (sodium extraction)
- Breathing air stored under pressure, renewed at the surface every 24 hours
- Diving apparatus: Rouquayrol system with copper helmet, modified by Nemo
- Underwater lighting: Ruhmkorff apparatus (electric lantern)
- Underwater gun: Air-powered, fires glass capsule bullets charged with electricity (lethal on contact)
- Food: Entirely from the sea -- fish, sea-weed tobacco, sea-weed sugar, turtle fillet, dolphin liver, anemone preserves

## Key Locations

### Aboard/At Sea
- **Pacific Ocean**: Initial hunting grounds (ch05-07), the submarine forest of Crespo Island (ch15-16), Torres Straits (ch19-20)
- **Indian Ocean**: Pearl banks of Manaar/Ceylon (ch25-26)
- **Red Sea**: Transit through the Arabian Tunnel (ch27-28)
- **Mediterranean**: Rapid 48-hour crossing (ch30), Grecian Archipelago where Nemo aids a diver/freedom fighter (ch29)
- **Atlantic Ocean**: Vigo Bay (treasure of sunken Spanish galleons, ch31), sunken Atlantis (ch32), Sargasso Sea (ch34)
- **Antarctic**: Journey south, through icebergs (ch36), reaching the South Pole (ch37), trapped under ice (ch38-39)
- **South Atlantic**: Cape Horn to the Amazon (ch40), giant squid attack (ch41)
- **North Atlantic**: Gulf Stream (ch42), final encounter with the warship (ch43-44)
- **Norwegian coast**: The maelstrom (ch45-46), escape to Lofoten Islands

### On Land
- **Island of Crespo** (ch15-16): First underwater walk in a submarine forest. Located in the North Pacific.
- **Papua New Guinea/Torres Straits** (ch19-21): The Nautilus runs aground. The trio goes ashore and encounters hostile natives. Nemo repels them with electrified hull.
- **The coral cemetery** (ch23): Underwater burial ground for Nemo's crew. Deep, solemn scene.
- **Manaar Bank, Ceylon** (ch26): Pearl diving expedition. Nemo saves an Indian pearl diver from a shark.
- **Vigo Bay, Spain** (ch31): Nemo harvests gold from sunken Spanish treasure galleons.
- **Atlantis** (ch32): Underwater ruins of the lost continent. Aronnax sees it by volcanic light.
- **The South Pole** (ch37): Nemo plants his black flag with a golden "N."
- **An extinct volcano/coal mine** (ch33): Nautilus refuels at an underwater coal deposit inside a submerged volcano.

## Modernization Decisions

1. **"Poulps"** --> "giant squid" (the famous squid attack chapter). More immediately recognizable.
2. **Ship names** should be italicized consistently: *Nautilus*, *Abraham Lincoln*, *Scotia*, *Moravian*, etc.
3. **Lengthy species catalogs**: Trim to the most vivid specimens. Readers do not need 40 species listed; they need to feel the overwhelming variety.
4. **"Master" for servants**: Conseil calling Aronnax "master" feels dated. Change to "sir" or "Professor" -- which he also uses in the original. Keep "Master Land" as Nemo's term for Ned (it carries deliberate condescension).
5. **French/Latin phrases**: Keep the most evocative ones (*Mobilis in Mobili*, *Nautron respoc lorni virch*) but translate or replace obscure ones.
6. **Conseil's third-person habit**: Keep but use sparingly -- it is charming in small doses.
7. **Currency and measurements**: Where the original gives specific amounts (the $2,000 reward, the 1,000-pound pearl), keep the original figures but do not add modern conversions mid-narrative. Footnotes can handle this.
8. **"M. Aronnax"**: Keep this form of address. "Monsieur" abbreviated to "M." is authentic and does not need modernizing.
